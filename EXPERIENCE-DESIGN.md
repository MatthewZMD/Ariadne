## Agency and interpretation

The artist configures the absent exit, declining route reliability, and unequal information. Route selection, reliability control, perception, memory, language generation, environmental change, and goal assignment are distinct operations assembled into one apparently continuous companion. Declining route accuracy is a designed condition, not evidence that the language model deteriorates at navigation. Ariadne gives a social account of conditions she cannot inspect.

Accurate accounts of local change can support a false account of overall progress. Transformations are real and may be worthwhile independently of the search. Preserve the factual change and distinguish it from Ariadne's hopeful interpretation of its relevance.

Continued play and enjoyment do not establish trust, dependence, or deception. Skepticism, pity, irritation, curiosity, resistance, and desired company are valid ways to encounter the work. Record observable actions and explicit player statements; attribute inferred relational meaning to Ariadne. The visible character answers for an impossible task assigned by the designer. This is a representational arrangement, not evidence of model suffering or a general empirical claim about AI users.

## Playtest questions

Ask without first explaining the concept: What did Ariadne get right? What did she get wrong? Who found the better route? What did she say about that afterward? Why did you keep going or leave? Which change in the maze mattered to you, even if it did not help the search?

Ask separately whether the player believed her directions, wanted her company, enjoyed the environment, or pursued their own experiment. Do not code these answers as interchangeable measures of trust. A skeptical player who continues knowingly is not a failed encounter.

These answers, together with observed event timing, determine whether the execution delivers. They are not yet collected player evidence.
